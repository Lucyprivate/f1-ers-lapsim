The MIT license covers project-owned source code and documentation only. It does not grant rights to third-party software, data, trademarks or official publications. Dependencies retain their respective licenses.

Official FIA regulation/event PDFs, historical telemetry, external track/source datasets, environment caches, large trajectories and experiment ZIP archives are omitted from this public repository. They are not relicensed under MIT. Source references and locally retained provenance do not replace the original providers' terms. The original local history and delivery archives remain preserved separately.

This is independent research and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Formula 1, the FIA or a racing team. The package implements a declared public-rule subset with explicit engineering approximations. It does not certify complete FIA compliance: non-public ECU period definitions, real driver-demand maps, transmission, battery SOC/losses and precise aero state remain unverified.

The 800 kg mass, 400 kW equivalent ICE wheel power and 2–4 MJ ideal-energy window are frozen modeling assumptions. Regeneration shares the existing negative wheel-demand budget with friction braking. Full regeneration demand does not imply unlimited recovery or extra braking capability.

The numerical result is the fastest eligible retained candidate in a finite experiment. It is not a proven global optimum or an accurate prediction of a real sustainable race lap. Accuracy gates failed and the registered FP2 test remains sealed.
